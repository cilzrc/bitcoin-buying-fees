## The payment method usually costs more than the trading fee

Here's the thing that trips up first-time buyers. Trading fees on major exchanges sit around 0.1%. Your bank's cut can be fifty times bigger.

Buy $1,000 of bitcoin with a Visa or Mastercard on Gate and the platform fee starts at 0.08% — genuinely cheap. But your issuing bank may add 2–5% on top for a crypto-related transaction, and if your card's currency differs from the settlement currency you'll eat an FX margin too. Gate's own guidance puts the all-in cost of a $1,000 card purchase somewhere around $1,020–$1,050 once bank charges are factored in. That's not Gate being expensive. That's the card network.

Peer-to-peer (Gate calls it P2P or C2C) flips this. Gate charges zero platform commission on P2P trades — both sides. You're dealing directly with another user who sets their own price and accepts a local payment method: bank transfer, Alipay, WeChat Pay, PayPal, or regional options depending on where you are. Payment channels differ a lot by country, and Gate's P2P market covers roughly 80 countries with 450+ channels.

The trade-off: the merchant's price is rarely the exact market price. It's usually a small premium or discount baked into their quote, which is how they make money with no platform fee. Compare a few ads before you commit. The best rates on P2P are often better than what a card processor gives you; the worst ones are worse.

## P2P safety: the four rules that matter

Gate holds the seller's bitcoin in escrow the moment you place a P2P order, and only releases it after the seller confirms your payment arrived. That escrow protects you from most of the classic scams. The remaining risk is on you.

1. Pay only to the account details shown inside the order page. Not to an account number someone sends you in chat, not to a "different account, same person" story. Gate's own guidance is blunt about this: off-platform payment details are how buyers lose money.
2. Keep the order inside Gate. Anyone asking you to move the conversation to Telegram, WhatsApp, or anywhere else is running a scam, whether they're the buyer or the seller.
3. Check reputation before you pick. Verified merchants carry blue-V or crown badges based on trading volume, completion rate, and compliance history. High volume and a 100% completion rate is a better signal than the cheapest price in the list.
4. Keep the receipt. Screenshot the transfer or save the bank confirmation. If a dispute opens, payment proof and the in-platform chat log are what Gate's support team reviews.

There's also a practical limit: P2P works best for small-to-mid-size purchases. Liquidity on any single ad is finite, and if you're trying to buy a very large amount in one go, the spot market with a limit order gives you better depth and predictable execution.
